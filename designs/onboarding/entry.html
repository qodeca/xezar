The setup entry

Two places, one action. The first-task hero is where a fresh project meets the offer; Settings → Project setup is where it still lives a month later. Both start the same ordinary task, and neither writes anything by itself.

1. Primary – the first-task hero on Tasks

The shipped hero is unchanged: the title, the sentence and the composer below. The design adds a quiet second block under a rule. A person who came here to work reads "Describe a task to get started.", types in the composer and never has to notice the rest.

Observed is not checked. "Last observed" is what is running now. Only a check that finished moves "Last successfully checked" — a partial or failed check leaves it where it was.

What "Set up this project" launches

  • An ordinary task, from the launch definition xezar bundles. No network is needed: the bundled copy is pinned and works offline.
  • Mode setup from this button; mode recheck from "Re-check now" and from the offer row.
  • The task asks only what it cannot work out — what this project is for, what it should produce, whether you work alone or through a leader — using the terminal question chips, two options each.
  • It prepares a per-file preview and writes nothing until a person accepts it. Every write stays inside the task's own worktree.
  • It ends with a plain list: the files it changed, the checks that really ran, what it did not do, and the steps that are still yours — starting your agent, signing in, connecting it.
  • Independent tasks are unaffected. Nothing in this design makes a normal task need setup, a network call or any configuration.
What the copy never says. It never tells you to take on xezar's own internal working files or its development process. It names your project's own agent instructions file, and it names the settings xezar itself reads. Anything beyond that is the task's own preview, in your words and under your control.
