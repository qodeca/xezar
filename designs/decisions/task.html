Task and leader

A case is born in a task. The task page shows the same card in the thread, says the task is stopped and its slot is free, and points at the Decisions page. The leader sees the case as an event and a refusal, never as something it can answer. The sample is #356, where the agent itself raised the case with XEZ:DECIDE in the middle of a step.

What the leader gets

The leader is an agent on the MCP. It sees the case, cannot answer it, and is told what to do instead: take the question to you, then record your words. No fifth tool: cases are read through task_read and changed through two execution_control actions. Shapes follow the existing journal rows, refusals, operationId and version tokens.

What changes on the task page

  • A new run state, decision. The task holds no slot, has no open session and arms no timer. lib/attention.ts gets its row: bucket “needs you”, tone pending, pulse, label “needs your decision”. The pill and the dot follow it.
  • A banner above the thread says the task is stopped and its slot is free, and links to Decisions. The review banner’s shape with the primary tone, because this is a question and not a review.
  • The card is the same component as on the Decisions page. Deciding in either place decides in both.
  • On a decision the task goes back to the queue and, when it gets a slot, resumes the agent with the context it had – or re-runs the step with a fresh agent when the session cannot be resumed. The decided card says which.
  • A Decisions tab joins Session, Changes and Files, with the count of this task’s open cases. One list: open cases, then “Decided · n”.
  • “Leader decides” on the card hands the case to the leader: the pill changes to “with the leader”, the task stays stopped, the composer stays disabled, and the banner reads “Stopped – the leader is deciding. Take it back on the card if you change your mind.”
  • The scope-check line shows the report-only diff check with its three-valued result.
  • The composer is disabled while a case is open, with the reason as its placeholder. Today a plain message resolves an agent’s question; here that would let the task slip past the gate.
